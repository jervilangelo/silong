Concept A · Combination mark · symbol + Newsreader wordmark

The Quiet Shelter

A low roof drawn at a 2:1 pitch. Its right eave steps down in three pixels, as if the rain running off it had turned to light.

Symbolism

Silong is the space under a roof. The mark draws only the roof, so the shelter is the empty space beneath it, where the reader stands. The stepped eave hints at rain dripping off the edge.

Typography

Newsreader (OFL), a serif made for long-form reading, set at its display optical size and weight 420. It is tracked slightly tight and keeps the full stop: silong. reads like a sentence that has ended, which is the pause.

Pixel influence

The 2:1 slope is the oldest pixel-art line there is: two across, one down. The three stairs on one eave are the only literal pixels. The rest of the roof is clean vector.

Colour

One colour: Paper on Midnight, Midnight on Paper. Neon can tint the roof in the nav. No accent needed.

Scalability

Weakest of the three at 16 px. The roof is wide and short, so it fills a square favicon poorly and the stairs blur away. It would need a taller, simplified favicon cut.

On the website

The roof can sit over section headers or a writer's profile like an awning. As a loading state it can draw its eave one step at a time.

Honest risk: At a glance the roof can read as a caret (^) or an “up” chevron, the common UI symbol.

Concept B · Wordmark with a custom tittle · Fraunces

The Falling Rain Recommended

The dot of the i is a raindrop: its left side a smooth pen curve, its right side resolved into three pixel steps.

Symbolism

Silong means shelter from rain, so a single drop lands in the name itself, where you'd expect an ordinary dot. Half drawn, half pixel: the page and the screen in one small shape, which is Silong's whole premise.

Typography

Fraunces (OFL) at weight 380 with softness 50, an old-style serif whose soft terminals feel warm and bookish, never corporate. Lowercase, no full stop: the drop is the one mark of punctuation.

Pixel influence

The smallest dose of the three. The steps sit on one flank of one drop, about 2% of the wordmark's ink. In the navigation it reads as a drop. Up close, the pixels show.

Colour

The wordmark in Paper (dark) or Midnight (light). The drop alone in Neon, or deep Mist on light backgrounds. It works fully in one colour too.

Scalability

The drop is the favicon and app icon. It holds at 16 px as a clear drop silhouette, and the stairs return from 32 px up.

On the website

The drop becomes the system: the cursor while reading, the “save draft” indicator, a bullet in poem lists, and a loader that falls once.

Honest risk: A drop on its own is a common symbol (water, utilities). The pixel flank and its place in the wordmark are what make it Silong's.

Concept C · Combination mark · symbol + Instrument Sans wordmark

The Illuminated Window

An arched window seen from the rainy street at night, with one warm pixel left on inside: someone is still up, writing.

Symbolism

Refuge from the reader's side: you're outside in the rain, and here is a lit room. The arch hints at old Filipino churches and capiz-shell windows without copying any of them.

Typography

Instrument Sans (OFL), a contemporary sans with humanist warmth, at weight 480. It is the most modern and app-like of the three. A serif could be swapped in if C wins but should feel more literary.

Pixel influence

Exactly one pixel, the lamp, set off-centre so it reads as a light in a room and not a keyhole. Everything else is a strict arc-and-line frame.

Colour

The only concept with a warm accent: frame in Paper or Midnight, lamp in amber (#E3A95C on dark, #C98A3A on light). In one colour the lamp becomes a solid square and still reads.

Scalability

Best at 16 px. The arch has a near-square footprint, and the lamp lands on a single physical pixel.

On the website

The lamp is a natural state light: on while someone is drafting, gently pulsing on an “open for submissions” page. The arch frames cover images.

Honest risk: A thick arch can be mistaken for a padlock or mailbox icon if the lamp moves to the centre, so its off-centre position must stay fixed.

Recommendation

Go with B, The Falling Rain.

It is the most literary and the least “logo-shaped”. In navigation it reads as a beautifully set name, and only up close do you find the drop and its pixel steps, which matches the 85/15 balance you asked for. It also gives a strong single-shape favicon, and the drop can carry the website's interactions without the brand ever looking like a game.

C is the strong alternative if you want a warmer, more symbolic presence and an amber accent. A is the most architectural but has the weakest small-size behaviour.
